# Resume quality spectrum

Assign the level from observable content. Use the target job. Give an uncertainty of ±1 level by default.

| Level | Label | Observable standard |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Unusable | Target, employers, dates, and relevant work cannot be reconstructed. Most text is unsupported self-description. |
| 2 | Very weak | A target and partial timeline exist, but duties and fit remain generic. |
| 3 | Weak | Timeline is readable and responsibilities are relevant, with little evidence of results or decisions. |
| 4 | Below average | Shows concrete scope or deliverables, but outcomes and ownership are missing. |
| 5 | Average | Has one meaningful result or case; measurement, attribution, or fit remains incomplete. |
| 6 | Qualified | Has at least two relevant cases and credible scope; some baselines, ownership, or decision reasoning are missing. |
| 7 | Good | Multiple relevant cases show actions, outcomes, and responsibility boundaries. The resume supports an interview for the target role. |
| 8 | Strong | Adds clear tradeoffs, comparable measurements, limitations, and a consistent professional pattern across roles. |
| 9 | Excellent | Highly tailored evidence covers nearly all core requirements, with strong decision depth, traceable measurement definitions, and no material narrative gap. |
| 10 | Near-perfect presentation | Meets level 9 and makes every major claim easy to audit from the resume: period, population, source, comparison, ownership, limitation, and relevance. Reserve this for rare documents; it does not certify truth. |

## Assignment rule

1. Identify the highest level whose complete standard is met.
2. Do not average unrelated strengths into a higher level. Strong formatting cannot replace missing evidence.
3. Use the lower level when a missing item changes the hiring decision; otherwise state a boundary such as `Level 7–8`.
4. Treat contact details, PDF layout, ATS parsing, and AI provenance as separate status checks unless they make the document unusable.
